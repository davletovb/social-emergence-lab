# Theoretical framework: micro-to-macro social emergence

## Core question
Under which explicitly specified interaction mechanisms do individual decisions, social histories, networks and institutions generate population-level patterns—and which competing mechanisms can be distinguished from the same observations?

A simulated regularity is **not** automatically evidence that real societies follow the same mechanism. Mechanism isolation, robustness, and comparison with empirical patterns are separate obligations.

## Mechanisms to contrast

These are a **research portfolio**, not interchangeable implemented features or claims of a common universal social law. The [current research portfolio](current-research-status.md) distinguishes what has actually been run from what is only a future direction.

- **Noise without reinforcement**: random opportunities whose allocation does not depend on prior outcomes, so chance differences do not change subsequent access to opportunities (baseline).
- **Cumulative advantage**: higher current rank or resources increase the chance of receiving later opportunities.
- **Reputation/memory**: past observed success affects peers' choices even where intrinsic ability is identical.
- **Network mediation and structural memory**: opportunity exposure depends on inherited links and replacement or turnover, not only own reputation. Implemented and explored in [Experiment 002](../experiments/002-reputation-lock-in/research-question.md); its V3 results favor slow relaxation under the tested settings.
- **Cooperation, norm and reinforcement mechanisms**: a common physical dyadic world with material fines, stable preferences, conditional cooperation, sorting, belief learning or contextual payoff reinforcement. The six-family [Experiment 003](../experiments/003-cooperation-mechanism-recovery/README.md) model and bounded exploratory pilot are implemented; the full population-law map and independent scientific verification are unfinished.
- **Institutional dynamics, social norms, polarization and simulation gaming**: candidate directions for separately designed future experiments, **not** completed empirical findings or approved study protocols.

## Assumptions to make explicit
Specify assumptions **per model**, not as universal properties of all experiments: population closure or turnover, fixed or mobile actors, initial resource distribution or individual preferences, measurement cadence, matching/fine assignment, event ordering, conservation or exogenous inflow, tie-breaking, resource floors and treatment of negative resources. For identification work also state the model-family nuisance hierarchy, allowed correlations, observable variables, time horizon and possible nested laws.

Potential theoretical connections include cumulative advantage (Matthew effects), path dependence, reinforcement processes, social learning, assortment, institutional feedback and stratification. These are candidate explanations to compare, not automatically distinct or recoverable families. These are starting hypotheses, not endorsements of a particular causal account.

## Causal scope
Randomly assigning simulation conditions can identify causal effects *within the specified computational model*. It does not establish causal effects in human societies without independent empirical validation and defensible mapping from modeled variables to observed social constructs. Further, **causal differences inside a simulation are not the same as observational identification**: different synthetic mechanism families can generate the same full public-history law, so the correct answer may be a set of compatible models rather than one label. [Experiment 003's open scientific gate](https://github.com/davletovb/social-emergence-lab/issues/65) includes independent proof review and valid uncertainty calibration.
