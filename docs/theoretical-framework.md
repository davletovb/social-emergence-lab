# Theoretical framework: micro-to-macro emergence

## Core question
Under which explicitly specified interaction mechanisms can initially symmetric individuals acquire persistently unequal outcomes?

A simulated regularity is **not** automatically evidence that real societies follow the same mechanism. Mechanism isolation, robustness, and comparison with empirical patterns are separate obligations.

## Mechanisms to contrast
- **Noise without reinforcement**: zero-mean shocks that do not change subsequent access to opportunities (baseline).
- **Cumulative advantage**: higher current rank or resources increase the chance of receiving later opportunities.
- **Reputation/memory**: past observed success affects peers' choices even where intrinsic ability is identical.
- **Network mediation (later extension)**: opportunity exposure depends on links, not only own history.

## Assumptions to make explicit
Population closure, fixed actors, identical initial resource endowment, no innate productivity differences, measurement cadence, event ordering, conservation or exogenous inflow, tie-breaking, resource floors, and treatment of negative resources.

Potential theoretical connections include cumulative advantage (Matthew effects), path dependence, reinforcement processes and stratification. These are starting hypotheses, not endorsements of a particular causal account.

## Causal scope
Randomly assigning simulation conditions can identify causal effects *within the specified computational model*. It does not establish causal effects in human societies without independent empirical validation and defensible mapping from modeled variables to observed social constructs.
