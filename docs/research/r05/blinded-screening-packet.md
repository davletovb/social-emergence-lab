## A. Inverse mechanism identification
**Variant A — cumulative inequality and opportunities.** Question: When different urn reinforcement, opportunity-network access and heterogeneous-quality generators exhibit the same macro inequality path, do shared randomized early-success, graph repair or neutral policy changes distinguish them? Required: identical operators available in every model, exact small transition baseline, common observation budget, honest overlapping regions. **Close studies:** [van de Rijt et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1316836111) *randomly bestowed early success across four online domains*; [Frey & van de Rijt 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38304) *arbitrary reputation inequality in a lab*; [Wilson & Collins 2019](https://elifesciences.org/articles/49547) model-recovery methodology. Existing lab 001/002 exact evidence is synthetic, not independent external validation.

**Variant B — cooperation mechanism recovery.** Question: Under common fine incentives and observable dyadic histories, can sanction response, personal norm preference, conditional cooperation, fixed-type partner assortment, mixed fine→belief learning and aspiration-reinforcement be distinguished with admissible lab-observation and assignment budgets? **Close studies:** [Galbiati et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1813502115) *randomly rematched dyadic prisoner's dilemma with fine per match*, [Engl et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1257/mic.20180336) *enforcement spillovers*, [Wilson & Collins 2019](https://elifesciences.org/articles/49547). Synthetic paired forks cannot masquerade as multiple outcomes from one human history. Empirical licensing/fields not yet audited. **Evaluate A and B at equal textual depth, do not assume either is preferred.**

## B. Hidden public conformity versus private preferences
Question: Can public-compliance paths generated by misperceived private consensus, accurate private beliefs but coordination/penalty motives, and true private agreement be told apart using *common truthful-disclosure and sanction manipulations*, conditional on what a real study could see? **Close work:** [Prentice & Miller 1993](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.64.2.243) *pluralistic ignorance*; [Centola et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aas8827) *social convention tipping*; [Bursztyn et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20180975) *randomized accurate-belief feedback and later actions*; [Lütz & Wardil 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2024.129920) *memory/pressure pluralistic-ignorance modeling*. An [openICPSR package](https://doi.org/10.3886/E119404V1) exists but fields/permissions and exact assignment not checked. No assumed novelty for truthful disclosure.

## C. Formation and resilience of sanction institutions
Question: For indistinguishable patterns of institutional entry, can a common *monitoring-cost perturbation* distinguish payoff incentives, legitimacy/choice preferences and cooperative-member self-selection? Complications: peer reward and punishment, group-size dependence, choice effect; same operator in all rivals. **Close work:** [Gürerk et al. 2006](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1123633), [Lo Iacono et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad091) (multilab institution-choice replication with costly peer punishment/reward), [Baldassarri & Grossman 2011](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1105456108). OSF study materials cited, file-variable and permissions unresolved. A simple no-choice two-actor oracle may miss institution legitimacy.

## D. Cooperation and renewable resources under disturbance
Question: Equal pre-shock cooperation but different reciprocal, enforced, pooled-risk and selectively shared extraction — do identical exogenous scarcity shocks produce diverging stock and behavioral recovery? Specify separate group resource rights, conservation and control, and absorbing/recoverable floors. **Close work:** [Anderies et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolecon.2013.04.010) irrigation-game uncertainty/asymmetry experiment; [Armstrong et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hydroa.2024.100188) institution emergence and salinity simulation; [Janssen & Anderies 2007](https://www.jstor.org/stable/26522981) robustness tradeoffs. Data rights not verified. Exact two-agent resource transition is possible under restrictive model rules.

## E. Polarization under bridging exposure
Question: With identical cross-group contact content, can sincere persuasion, defensive identity updating, and selective-exposure rewiring have the same aggregate attitude trajectory but different individual responses? Use the same assigned opposing-view exposure manipulation in all generators and distinguish individual belief from recorded feed exposure. **Close work:** [Bail et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1804840115) randomized social-media opposing-view exposure; [replication materials](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/NSESEH) indexed with CC0 and variable coarsening for privacy. File-level longitudinal assignment fields are not yet audited; direct human behavior claims cannot be made from simulated contacts.

## F. Referral networks versus screening gatekeeping
Question: Can unequal referral-access opportunity, selective conditional screening and genuine match-quality heterogeneity explain the same applicant/hire statistics, and would a common randomized *referral opportunity offer* separate them? Include the missing never-applied population and a no-referral null. **Close work:** [Petersen, Saporta & Seidel 2000](https://doi.org/10.1086/318961), 35,229 applicants and referral-adjusted race effects; [Fernandez, Castilla & Moore 2000](https://doi.org/10.1086/210432), different referral explanations; [Castilla 2008](https://doi.org/10.1086/588738), performance-adjusted organizational returns. An observational referral adjustment is not a randomized offer; need a more exact new contrast. Empirical at-risk network/recruitment data licensing unknown.
