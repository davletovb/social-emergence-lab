## Candidate questions (no known studies, no rankings)

**A1 — Inequality and opportunity access.** Distinct individual quality, positive feedback/cumulative success and opportunity-network mechanisms produce the same observed inequality trajectory. Is there a common externally randomized early-opportunity or posthistory graph intervention, with independent null model, that separates them? What if some remain structurally observationally equivalent?

**A2 — Cooperation, incentives and sorting.** In repeated social dilemmas, can fine sensitivity, personal normative preferences, conditional responses to others, fixed-type assortative matching, enforcement-induced belief learning and payoff-aspiration updating be distinguished using observable action and assignment histories? Avoid falsely treating counterfactual forks as observations from one real human group.

**B — Hidden private disagreement.** Public agreement can coexist with private dissent, coordination payoffs and misestimated peers' attitudes. Which standardized truthful private-attitude-disclosure and **separately defined removal of sanctions** could distinguish these accounts if private attitudes, public compliance and intervention exposure are separately observable? When would they remain equivalent?

**C — Institution adoption and persistence.** Two group-formation mechanisms generate equal rates of adopting enforcement institutions: a material cost/benefit mechanism and a preference for institutional choice/legitimacy, potentially confounded by membership composition. Can the same externally imposed **increase in institution-level monitoring cost** distinguish them (with the payer and accounting rules fixed identically across mechanisms)?

**D — Cooperation after scarcity.** Sharing based on reciprocal obligations, cost-backed enforcement, risk pooling or selective access produces equal pre-shock extraction/cooperation. Can an identical, externally introduced resource disturbance distinguish postshock behavioral and stock recovery, with explicit conservation and access rights?

**E — Polarization and cross-group contact.** Persuasive updating, defensive identity response and endogenous selective exposure can yield the same aggregate opinions. Does a common randomized cross-group exposure and independently recorded pre/post attitudes distinguish them, conditional on content and assignment effects?

**F — Referral and selection.** Unequal referral-opportunity access, employer screening preferences and applicant qualification variation can produce similar hiring disparities. Can an exogenous opportunity-to-refer intervention and direct observation of *who could have applied* distinguish these mechanisms?
