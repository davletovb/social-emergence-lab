# R-05 dossier 6 — Mobility and organizational gatekeeping
**Date:** 2026-10-24 · **Stage:** Tier-1 screen / potential shortlist

## Narrow question and contribution
Can the same unequal promotion/hiring trajectory be generated by (a) **unequal access to referral channels**, (b) **conditional screening/selection bias after application**, and (c) **genuine heterogeneous match quality**, and can a **common exogenous referral-opportunity equalization** distinguish the mechanisms? Claim: synthetic causal identifiability / exact failure region first, not proof of discriminatory intent in a real employer. For human claims, need full at-risk eligible applicant pool, referral exposure/connection, actual application, selection stage, qualifications and independent hiring decisions.

## Symmetric intervention / oracle / negative result
Define a common opportunity-supply operator: exogenously offer qualified candidates identical referral access without directly changing their quality, evaluator preference or information. Compare *who applies* versus *conditional selection probabilities* and eventual pay. All rival generators must accept that same operator, and nulls must cover equal quality, fair selection and no referrals. A two-step small state-space recruiter/applicant model can give exact expected-access and transition probabilities; identity-based labels should be treated as experimental randomized tags in synthetic models, **not** claims about protected real groups. Known overlap: unobserved candidate quality and selective referrals may yield the same observed hire rate as bias if only offers are visible.

## Original-source Tier-1 scan
- **Petersen, Saporta & Seidel (2000)**, *Offering a Job: Meritocracy and Social Networks*, American Journal of Sociology, https://doi.org/10.1086/318961 — **publisher original abstract checked**, analyzing **35,229 applicants** over 1985–94. Network referral method accounts for a portion of between-group differences, cautioning that disparity may occur *before* employer evaluation. Full person-level data/rights are **not** known accessible.
- **Fernandez, Castilla & Moore (2000)**, *Social Capital at Work: Networks and Employment at a Phone Center*, AJS, https://doi.org/10.1086/210432 — **original publisher abstract checked**, distinguishes “richer pool,” “better match,” and “social enrichment” referral mechanisms; names already widely established rival explanations.
- **Castilla (2008)**, *Gender, Race, and Meritocracy in Organizational Careers*, AJS, https://doi.org/10.1086/588738 — **publisher original abstract checked**; bias can persist in reward/promotion conditional on recorded performance ratings. Not interchangeable with pre-application network disadvantage.

## Feasibility and selection
Analytical toy and transparent Hiyal world potentially inexpensive, but **real organizational at-risk/referral records are not verified accessible or reusable**; an employer aggregate hire table cannot establish network access or bias. Sharp substantive distinction is worthwhile even if result is non-identification when only selected applicants are measured. Distinctive risk: overlap with E002 structural-opportunity work must be explicitly acknowledged and a new design cannot just rename an old synthetic graph intervention. **Status: provisionally eligible for Tier 2 (synthetic methods claim only)**; reopen/stop if prior art and E002 already exhaust exact access/selection contrast.
