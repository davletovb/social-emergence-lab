## Closest-predecessor correction (2026-10-24)
**[van de Rijt, Kang, Restivo & Patil (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1316836111)**, original PNAS published abstract/significance and original methods description: in four online domains, **randomly granted early success** leads to later higher success than untreated controls. This is *already* an experimental discrimination between cumulative feedback and unobserved-ability-only accounts. Thus “randomized early-success vs quality” is **not an untested novel idea**. It is a **common operator** our proposed generators can accept, but the dossier must specify *additional* mechanism comparisons and outcomes absent from that experiment.

**[Frey & van de Rijt (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38304)**, original published abstract: a laboratory exchange/reputation system with ex ante equally trustworthy agents produced arbitrary exchange inequality under reputation feedback. Our proposed A is close to both this established result and E002's own access-memory results. Detailed methodological nonduplication is **unresolved**.

**Variant B (#36):** its dyadic fine/partner-sorting methodology remains a separate substantive candidate, but comparator assignment and latent observations must not be conflated with human lab data. Both A and B will be presented at **equal synopsis length and source depth** to the next independent dossier-only reviewer. Neither gets a predeclared burden of proof to replace the other.

**Possible disconfirmers:** earlier work already tests a common intervention and arbitrary reputation inequality. **What would create a specific research gap?** A prewritten simultaneous alternative-model prediction for an intervention and a diagnostic pattern *not covered* by the previous studies, or a mathematically informative observational-equivalence boundary under realistic observables. No discovery claim at Tier 1.
