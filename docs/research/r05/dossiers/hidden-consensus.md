# R-05 dossier 2 — Hidden consensus and norm collapse
**Date:** 2026-10-24 · **Stage:** Tier-1 source scan / candidate, not R-06

## Narrow question and contribution
When can near-identical *public agreement* conceal different distributions of *private disagreement*, and which intervention (accurate disclosure of peers' actual private beliefs vs changing sanction risk) separates a **pluralistic-ignorance mechanism** from a **public coordination/payoff mechanism**? Not “discover a universal tipping percentage.” **Claim type:** synthetic mechanism-discrimination first; direct human mechanism inference would require independently measured private attitudes, public choices and assignment/timing.

## Competing mechanisms and common interventions
(1) Privately dissenting agents comply publicly because they misperceive others' private beliefs; (2) agents accurately anticipate a norm but comply because public deviation is costly/coordination-sensitive; (3) persistent apparent agreement from true preference consensus (null). In every rival, define the **same physical operator**: truthful private-belief distribution feedback with identical disclosure format, and a separately predeclared sanction-removal manipulation. A public norm can persist under all three; single-wave agreement is **not diagnostic**. A simple 2–4-agent Bayesian/threshold oracle must specify private/public state and information visibility. An exact common trajectory could remain observationally equivalent until private beliefs are elicited; record that limitation as a result rather than converting private states into observable data.

## Original-source Tier-1 scan
- **Prentice & Miller (1993)**, *Pluralistic ignorance and alcohol use on campus*, Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.64.2.243 — **authors' original abstract examined** via Princeton/Stanford institutional publication records. Four studies compare own attitudes and perceived peers' attitudes; their findings already show misperceived norm and attitude adaptation. Access to individual-level responses and permissible reuse **not verified**.
- **Centola, Becker, Brackbill & Baronchelli (2018)**, *Experimental evidence for tipping points in social convention*, Science, https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aas8827 — **original published abstract examined**. Their lab coordination experiment studies a critical mass for changing convention, not direct observation of privately rejected norms. A “25% tipping threshold” discovery claim would conflict with prior art; external universality unsupported.
- **2024 counterprior:** *The evolution of pluralistic ignorance*, Physica A, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2024.129920 — publisher abstract located; includes imitation/memory and social pressure, so even a private/public modeling mechanism is established. Its full methods and novelty boundaries require Tier 2.

## Feasibility / evidence access
**Required for empirical claim:** true privately elicited attitudes, perceived group beliefs, public compliance, intervention assignment, participant/group independence and measurement timing; these are not licensed or verified available as raw records. **Synthetic narrow contribution:** prove an identification/nonidentification frontier under disclosure vs sanction conditions, not “social consensus is fragile.” **Negative null:** accurate beliefs + no sanctions produce same public action as misinformation; check whether the claim is only weak inference from unobserved internals. A small exact oracle is cheap; population-level human claims remain out of scope. **Current screening:** provisionally eligible for deeper check, especially prior-art overlap with 1993 and 2024; no numerical score.
