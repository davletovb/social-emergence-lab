# R-05 dossier 5 — Polarization and bridging relationships
**Date:** 2026-10-24 · **Updated:** 2026-10-25 · **Status:** Tier-1 source-corrected research dossier; **UNDER RE-SCREEN**, no approved shortlist or scores

## Narrow question and claim
Given fixed *observed* cross-group exposures, can **persuasion/assimilation** and **identity-threat/defensive updating** generate the same aggregate polarization yet respond differently to exogenously introduced cross-group contacts with matched content? A third rival is **selective exposure/network rewiring** with no direct attitude resistance. **Claim class:** synthetic identifiability first; empirical comparison only with treatment-specific longitudinal attitudes and exposure assignment — not just network graph snapshots.

## Common intervention / overlaps
Same physical randomized exposure treatment and identical content/information delivery for every generator, with a separate control for exposure choice. Track individual pre/post attitude change, outgroup exposure, engagement and dropout. Unobserved messaging tone/credibility and partisan asymmetry could confound effects. A two-person signed-opinion update toy is tractable; yet polarity scales and measurement noise must be interpreted carefully. Similar aggregate distributions may mask who moved or disengaged; a negative result could show aggregate equivalence but distinguishable within-agent direction.

## Original-source Tier-1 scan
- **Bail et al. (2018)**, *Exposure to opposing views on social media can increase political polarization*, PNAS, https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1804840115 — **original full-text significance/abstract/methods/data-availability sections checked**. Their randomized compensated Twitter-bot follow experiment yielded different responses by political group (Republican participants more conservative, Democratic point estimate small and not statistically significant). A blanket claim that bridging contact reduces polarization is directly contradicted by this experimental context.
- **Existing replication/data statement** links Harvard Dataverse at https://dataverse.harvard.edu/dataverse.xhtml?alias=chris_bail — **the paper's link was checked, not the underlying Dataverse files, assignment-level variables or their reuse rights**.
- Competing prior network-vs-content causal designs and their close literature still need Tier 2; there is no valid inference that one PNAS field treatment precisely isolates identity threat.

## Feasibility / decision
Prospectively recreating a social-media field treatment is ethically/platform-sensitive, high dimensional and likely costlier than small synthetic model recovery. Available archived treatment-specific attitude/exposure measures may enable a bounded methodological benchmark **only after** variable-level audit. **Status: UNDER RE-SCREEN.** The earlier deferral for unavailable human empirical microdata was inconsistent with accepting **synthetic-only** evidence from other candidates. Human-data compatibility is a *separate claim-specific question*, not a synthetic research gate.


## Evidence availability and fairness correction (2026-10-24)
Bail et al. (2018) has an [identified public Harvard Dataverse deposit](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/NSESEH), whose indexed record currently reports a **CC0 1.0 metadata license** and warns that some social-media-linked variables were **coarsened for respondent privacy**. This improves the *potential* empirical route, but we have **not inspected the participant-level data dictionary, intervention observables, linkage/granularity, file-level terms or statistical disclosure effects**. A direct empirical replication cannot presume those fields are sufficient for identity-threat inference.

A **synthetic rival-model discrimination** study of persuasion, defensive updating and selection remains potentially viable **even without** participant-level files. The old gate treated the lack of an audited empirical dataset as fatal here but not for other explicitly synthetic dossiers; that inconsistency is withdrawn. Compare its *specific* new differentiating intervention against the 2018 trial and follow-up mechanism studies using the **same closest-source depth**. Re-screen pending.

## Review 3 — more source-based alternatives (2026-10-25)
- [Levy (2021), *AER*](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20191777) randomized ideologically different Facebook news subscriptions; his abstract reports more cross-view news visits, *less negative out-party attitudes*, but **no detectable change in political opinions**. It is **incorrect** to aggregate this and other exposure tests as universally null.
- [Nyhan et al. (2023), *Nature*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06297-w) reports a feed-ranking intervention with little detected attitude/polarization effect in that particular design, not a general impossibility.
- [de Jong (2024), *Communications Psychology*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44271-023-00051-8) tested assigned cross-partisan conversations and reports increased sympathy under its conditions; this is a different operator than algorithmic exposure.
These original publication abstracts/methods snippets were consulted, **not replication microdata**. Bail's [Harvard Dataverse DOI 10.7910/DVN/NSESEH](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/NSESEH) was confirmed through its [NLM dataset catalog record](https://datasetcatalog.nlm.nih.gov/dataset?q=0000080928). A CC0 catalog statement and anonymization/coarsening disclosure do not validate variable availability or mechanism identification. Synthetic and empirical routes must be screened separately.
