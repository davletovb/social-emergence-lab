## Verified source corrections (original journal/repository metadata, not our synthetic results)

1. Inequality identifiability: **van de Rijt, Kang, Restivo & Patil (2014)**, PNAS [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1316836111), already randomized early success across online domains and measured subsequent success. **Frey & van de Rijt (2016)**, Scientific Reports [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38304), already reported artificial inequality in a reputation-mediated lab exchange. Generic early-success manipulation is no novel gap.
2. Hidden consensus: **Bursztyn, González & Yanagizawa-Drott (2020)**, AER [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20180975), already corrects private-norm misperceptions with randomly supplied truthful information and measures behavioral consequences. The authors’ [openICPSR replication package](https://doi.org/10.3886/E119404V1) is indexed, not audited for adequate observable data/rights.
3. Referral/gatekeeping: **Petersen, Saporta & Seidel (2000)**, AJS [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/318961), reports referral-adjusted group effects and a pre-contact network disadvantage; therefore the broad referral-vs-later-screening distinction is a **direct close precedent**. Differentiate a truly exogenous referral-offer operator from this conditional observational analysis.
4. Polarization: **Bail et al. (2018)**, PNAS [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1804840115), includes a [Harvard Dataverse deposit](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/NSESEH); indexed catalog metadata reports **CC0 and privacy-related coarsened variables**. Actual file/variable and data permissions still require examination. Synthetic-first polarization cannot be excluded for absent human data while other synthetic topics pass.
5. Institution formation: relevant [Lo Iacono et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1093/pnasnexus/pgad091) OSF link is identifiable, though view-only/archive links are **not a license**. Existing institutional effects do not automatically settle a distinct proposed cost-perturbation identification boundary.
6. Scarcity: **Anderies, Janssen, Lee & Wasserman**, published version **2013**, [Ecological Economics 93:166–176](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolecon.2013.04.010), not “2012 published article” (an earlier working/repository version). Resource uncertainty/asymmetric access have already been experimentally investigated.
7. Correct authors: **Alessandra F. Lütz and Lucas Wardil (2024)**, *The evolution of pluralistic ignorance*, Physica A [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physa.2024.129920). Mechanism models with social pressure/imitation/memory precede a naive new toy.
