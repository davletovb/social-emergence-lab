## Findings genuinely important to scientific novelty

1. **A1 formal twin result is directly relevant.** [Gelastopoulos, Sage & van de Rijt, *Inferring cumulative advantage from longitudinal records* (2023 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01096) proves a talent/path-dependence **longitudinal observational-equivalence** result. This materially weakens novelty of a generic “two generators produce indistinguishable histories” study; the novelty candidate must instead be a **nontrivial graph intervention or a boundary with a different observation scheme**. We verified the existence and central theorem claim from the arXiv source abstract, **not every theorem index or proof in the DeepSeek report**. **Caution:** a *degree-preserving* rewire does **not generally equalize degree-based opportunity**; if mechanism advantage is based on degree, node degrees remain the same by definition. The proposed “token independent of graph in a talent model” and “effects vanish under rewiring in a network model” signatures require explicit model assumptions, not generic truths.
2. **A2 genuine comparator:** [Yang & Yue (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-50083-6) experimentally manipulated history-weighted assortative matching versus random matching. DeepSeek's *Scientific Reports* **article number 14850 is wrong**: original journal and PubMed records say **9:13609**. The underlying matching result is real; six-family identification is **not** thereby demonstrated, nor is a small “sub-threshold” fine shift guaranteed to distinguish mechanisms.
3. **B implementation drift:** the official dossier/neutral packet defines a **two-factor operator**: truthful disclosure of a private-belief distribution × **actual sanction removal**; private-attitude elicitation is a **measurement step in both/all arms**. DeepSeek's B pre-search description instead says “incentivised private-attitude elicitation crossed with ... sanction removal,” and elsewhere returns to aggregate disclosure × sanction removal. *Eliciting* private attitudes and *randomly disclosing truthful peer information* are different interventions. Also anonymous audience, sanctions, and perceived sanction risk are different levers. Fix that discrepancy **before** borrowing its B evidence-feasibility verdict. [Smerdon, Offerman & Gneezy (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10683-019-09616-z) already contrasts pluralistic-ignorance versus coordination behavior experimentally; DeepSeek B did not foreground this direct earlier comparator.
4. **C cost vs legitimacy:** [Dickson, Gordon & Huber (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abj7377) already randomizes aspects of fairness/enforcement accuracy to identify legitimacy vs material channels. It has [replication archives](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/SPIMAV) and [a Yale Dataverse version](https://doi.org/10.60600/YU/6MVZSU) (catalog metadata show public files but custom terms). The DeepSeek asserted cost/legitimacy algebra is **a hypothesis under its specific utility restrictions**, not a universal theorem. Absorbing a legitimacy term in an adoption threshold is not on its own a newly identified sociological mechanism. Its “no material unknowns beyond standard lab ethics” claim is **overconfident** about observable legitimacy, intervention validity and access rights.
5. **D/E/F experimental expansions are alternate candidates unless explicitly approved:** D adds an idiosyncratic announced shock **and randomized access rights** to the dossier's fixed common stock shock; E adds source similarity/forced-vs-free exposure to the original randomized-content question; F suggests independently advancing an eligible candidate pool beyond original opportunity-to-refer randomization. These may be interesting **future design amendments**, but their extra discriminating power cannot retroactively upgrade the original proposal in the R-05 comparison. Require a fresh, equivalently detailed dossier and same all-six comparison if any amendment becomes the nominated question.
6. **E/F empirical optimism** rests on hypothetical ethical, field-data and randomization access not demonstrated in this lab's current evidence route. [Srivastava's referral intervention](https://kartiksrivastava94.github.io/pages/Srivastava_JobReferrals.pdf) is real and close, making generic F novelty demanding; DeepSeek's F did not foreground it. Randomizing referral invitations does not identify unobserved applicant skill, preference or employer evaluation by itself. For E, forced-vs-voluntary exposure can introduce choice-selection differences, but cannot be called identified “defensive identity” without valid auxiliary measures and shared content/attention constraints.
